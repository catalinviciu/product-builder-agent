# Track B: Non-Powered & Off-Road Asset Market Sizing (US)
### Fleet/Asset Management Software — Vertical Ranking by Assets Under Management
Prepared September 2026. Scope: non-powered and off-road assets only (trailers/containers, heavy & compact construction equipment, forklifts/MHE, ag equipment, portable/site assets, utility/telecom field assets, medical mobile assets, returnable transport items). Powered on-road vehicles (trucks, vans, cars, buses) are covered by a parallel researcher and excluded here.

---

## 1. How to read this report

- **Confidence labels**: **High** = named primary source (federal statistical agency, trade association with published methodology, SEC filing). **Medium** = industry-research-firm estimate with visible methodology (Berg Insight, IPAF, AEM) or a reasoned extrapolation from a High-confidence input, clearly marked as *estimate*. **Low** = vendor blog / SEO market-research aggregator (marketsandmarkets, market.us, IMARC, GMI, IBISWorld, etc.) with no visible methodology — these are directionally useful but should not be treated as ground truth.
- Where I could not find a number at all, I wrote **"not found"** rather than inventing one.
- All dollar and unit figures are US unless stated otherwise.

## 2. Buyer-type framework: rental/leasing companies vs. owner-operators (read this before the ranked tables)

Per scope refinement, this report treats **equipment rental/leasing companies** and **owner-operator verticals** as two structurally distinct segment types — not one collapsed "who's the real buyer" answer. Both are real software buyers with opposite economics, and a follow-on team researching each segment's KPIs needs to know which one it's dealing with:

| | **Rental/Leasing companies** | **Owner-operators** |
|---|---|---|
| What the asset is to them | The product itself — the thing they sell access to | A cost input to producing something else (a building, a harvest, a delivered load, a barrel of oil) |
| Primary KPIs | Time utilization %, dollar utilization %, rental rate achieved vs. book/OEC, fleet age, return on invested capital, "getting assets off the yard" | Cost per operating hour, uptime/availability when a crew needs it, theft/shrinkage avoidance, avoiding over-buying (idle owned capital) |
| Typical asset count per company | Very large (single companies can hold tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands of units) | Ranges from a handful (small contractor/farm) to thousands (large GC, national waste hauler) |
| Software buyer role | The buyer **is** the asset owner | The buyer **is** the asset owner **and** the operator (same entity) — distinct from rental, where owner ≠ end user |
| Named examples in this research | United Rentals, Sunbelt Rentals, Herc Rentals, Ahern, BlueLine (construction/MEWP/tool rental); TRAC Intermodal, DCLI, Flexi-Van, XTRA Lease (trailer/chassis leasing & pooling) | Contractors/GCs (construction), carriers/private fleets (freight), 3PLs/manufacturers (forklifts), farms (agriculture), utilities, waste haulers, E&P operators, mining companies, hospitals |

**Where the same physical asset appears in both tables (the visible double-count):** an excavator owned by United Rentals and rented to a general contractor for a 6-month job shows up in the Rental/Leasing table (§3) as part of United Rentals' fleet, *and* that same unit is operationally in use inside the Construction owner-operator's world (§4) for the duration of the rental — but it is not owned by, and does not appear in, the contractor's own asset register. I have **not** summed the two tables into one grand total for this reason: doing so would double-count every rented unit. Where a vertical's split between "rented-in" and "owned" equipment could not be estimated (true for nearly every category studied), I say so explicitly rather than guessing a percentage.

A parallel structural point: **trailer/chassis leasing (TRAC, DCLI, Flexi-Van, XTRA Lease) is economically a rental/leasing business** even though "trucking" is conventionally described as an owner-operator industry — chassis in particular are overwhelmingly pooled/leased rather than carrier-owned, so I have placed chassis under Rental/Leasing (§3) and carrier-owned trailers under Freight owner-operators (§4), rather than lumping "Freight & Intermodal" into a single row as the original draft did.

## 5. TOP 5 recommendation (software-addressable, not raw population)

Raw population (if the two tables above were summed without regard to double-counting, and Returnable Transport Items were included) would be dominated by pallets/cylinders/kegs — excluded here because their per-unit economics don't support individual tracking (see §7). Re-ranked for *software-addressable opportunity*, and explicitly tagged by buyer type per the scope refinement:

1. **Freight, Trucking & Intermodal — [mixed: OWNER-OPERATOR for trailers, RENTAL/LEASING for chassis].** The single largest software-addressable asset class in scope: an estimated 3.5–4.5M carrier-owned trailers (owner-operator buyer) plus 700,000+ intermodal chassis that are overwhelmingly pooled/leased (TRAC, DCLI, Flexi-Van — rental/leasing buyer), with a Berg Insight-measured tracking penetration of only ~20% across the combined population. Best-evidenced vertical in this study (Berg Insight publishes a dedicated report on exactly this asset class) with the deepest vendor bench: Samsara, ORBCOMM, SkyBitz, BlackBerry Radar, Spireon/Solera, Tive, project44.

2. **Equipment Rental & Leasing Companies (construction, MEWP, tool rental) — [RENTAL/LEASING].** United Rentals, Sunbelt, Herc, Ahern and the long tail of independents, plus a ~857,000-unit US MEWP rental fleet (IPAF 2024). This buyer's economics (utilization, book-value recovery, loss write-offs) are the cleanest fit for asset-tracking software's core value proposition, and the NER/NICB theft-loss estimate of **$300M–$1B/year** falls disproportionately on this buyer as direct fleet write-offs. Vendor bench: Tenna, Trackunit, EquipmentShare (which itself sits astride both rental and contractor use cases), Samsara.

3. **Construction Owner-Operators (contractors, specialty trades) — [OWNER-OPERATOR], distinct from #2.** The same physical equipment population as #2 overlaps here only for the rented-in share of a contractor's equipment; this row is specifically the contractor-*owned* equipment plus the contractor's own tools. This buyer cares about job-cost allocation, theft/shrinkage, and utilization of capital they've tied up in owned iron — a related but genuinely different software motion (job-costing/allocation-oriented) from #2's yard/fleet-utilization motion. Vendor bench: Tenna, HCSS, B2W, Hilti ON!Track, Milwaukee ONE-KEY.

4. **Warehousing, Logistics/3PL & Manufacturing (forklifts/MHE) — [OWNER-OPERATOR].** A population plausibly in the 2.5–3.5M unit range (my estimate; no authoritative current ITA installed-base figure found), with meaningfully lower telematics penetration than trailers or heavy construction iron — forklifts are a historically under-instrumented category, and this buyer owns rather than rents at scale, unlike construction equipment. Vendors: Zonar, Position Imaging, Samsara, plus OEM telematics from Toyota/Crown/Hyster.

5. **Agriculture — [OWNER-OPERATOR].** 1.9M US farms and a multi-million-unit population of tractors, combines and implements (exact current total not confirmed — flagged as a data gap; see §8). Penetration is sharply bifurcated: large row-crop operations running new equipment are heavily instrumented (JDLink), while the majority of the installed base (older/smaller equipment, smaller farms) is not — a two-speed market a product team should treat as two sub-segments, not one.

**Verticals deliberately excluded from the Top 5 despite large raw counts or notable characteristics:**
- **Returnable Transport Items** (largest raw population of any category studied, ~2.6B pallets alone) — excluded because the economics genuinely don't support per-unit GPS/cellular tracking (§7); this is a pooling/RFID business (CHEP, etc.), not a fleet/asset-management SaaS opportunity today, regardless of whether the buyer would be framed as rental/leasing (the pool operator) or owner-operator (the shipper).
- **Portable Sanitation & Waste-Container Rental** (~1.9M portable toilets alone, RENTAL/LEASING-type buyer) — a real candidate for a 6th slot given it is the *least-penetrated* category found in this research (no tracking-penetration figure exists at all), but demoted below the Top 5 because, unlike #1–#5, no named vendor evidence surfaced of software companies actively selling into this specific buyer today — the commercial market for tracking software here looks nascent/unproven rather than merely under-penetrated.
- **Healthcare facilities** — excluded because, even though the asset count may rival some Top-5 verticals, the buyer and software category (clinical engineering / RTLS vendors like CenTrak, Stanley Healthcare) are structurally distinct from industrial fleet/asset management, and the evidence base here (bed counts, not device counts) doesn't translate cleanly into a comparable asset count.

## 7. Software-addressable vs. total population — where the economic floor sits

The prompt correctly anticipates that tag economics create a hard floor, and this floor applies **regardless of buyer type** (rental company or owner-operator — the physics of a $60/year cellular tag against a $300 tool don't change based on who owns the tool). Based on publicly known asset-tag pricing (Samsara Asset Tag / Asset Tag XS, and comparable BLE/cellular trackers typically running $50–$150 hardware + $50–$100/year subscription per tag, per vendor product pages), a rough rule of thumb from this research:

- **Assets worth $2,000+ and/or theft-prone/high-utilization-value** (trailers, heavy equipment, MEWPs, forklifts, mid-to-large ag equipment) clear the economic floor easily — tracking cost is a rounding error against asset value or against a single theft/loss event. This is where essentially all named vendors in this report concentrate, for both rental/leasing and owner-operator buyers.
- **Assets worth roughly $200–$2,000** (power tools, small compressors/generators, attachments) are a contested middle zone — this is exactly the segment Milwaukee ONE-KEY (free, Bluetooth-crowdsourced) and Hilti ON!Track (subscription, bundled with tool-fleet-as-a-service leasing contracts — itself a rental/leasing-type buyer) have targeted with *lower-cost* tracking models rather than standard cellular GPS trackers, because a $60/year cellular tag genuinely does not pencil out against a $300 tool.
- **Assets worth under ~$200** (most hand tools, individual pallets, gas cylinders, kegs) are **not** economically trackable as individual GPS/cellular assets at any plausible current hardware cost — hence returnable transport items are managed via pooling (CHEP-style deposit/exchange systems, itself a third ownership model distinct from both rental/leasing and owner-operator) or bulk RFID at the pallet-load level, not per-unit tracking.

I was not able to find a published, named source that states this economic-floor logic explicitly as an industry rule of thumb — it is presented here as a **reasoned estimate**, built from the observable pricing of named products (Samsara asset tags, Milwaukee ONE-KEY, Hilti ON!Track) rather than from a market-research citation, and should be labeled as such if reused.

## 8. Commercial reality: utilization/shrinkage vs. compliance/driver-behavior

Every source touching construction, rental, and intermodal assets in this research converged on **theft, shrinkage, and utilization** — not driver behavior or regulatory compliance (the dominant framing for powered on-road vehicles) — as the stated commercial driver. Notably, this driver splits cleanly along the buyer-type line drawn in §2:
- For **rental/leasing buyers**, the dominant story is **utilization**: MEWP fleet utilization was reported at **71% in 2024** (IPAF) — utilization-rate benchmarking is the primary value proposition here, because this buyer's revenue is literally a function of asset-days rented. "Asset management" software sells on **revenue-per-asset-day**, not safety/compliance, for this buyer.
- For **owner-operator buyers** (especially construction contractors), the dominant story is **theft/shrinkage**: NER/NICB estimate US construction-equipment theft costs **$300M–$1B/year**, with **<25% recovery rate** (the range itself, not a single point estimate, is the citable fact). A single vendor case anecdote (Samsara customer story, Low confidence) reported **$120K in avoided replacement cost in one year** and recovery of 3 forklifts in 3 months after deploying asset tags, protecting an "$80M equipment fleet" — illustrative of the shrinkage narrative for an owner-operator, but not a generalizable statistic.
- Berg Insight's framing of cargo-carrying-unit tracking explicitly centers on **utilization and shrinkage/loss of trailers and containers**, not driver behavior — consistent with the trailer/container use case being about the asset, not a person operating it (there frequently is no operator at all — an unpowered trailer sitting in a yard).

This is a structurally different sales narrative than powered-vehicle fleet software (ELD/HOS compliance, driver scorecards, insurance telematics), and a product team should expect a different buyer persona for each buyer type: a **rental operations / fleet manager** persona (utilization-focused) for Rental/Leasing companies, and an **equipment manager / loss-prevention or job-cost** persona (shrinkage- and cost-focused) for Owner-Operators — not a safety/compliance officer in either case.

## 9. Taxonomy and double-counting — summary

**Taxonomy used in this report.** Assets are organized first by **buyer type** (Rental/Leasing vs. Owner-Operator, §2–§4), and within each, by owning/operating industry vertical, per the assignment. Where one asset class (e.g., trailers) is used across multiple verticals (trucking companies, 3PLs, manufacturers with private fleets), it is attributed to the vertical where the software purchase decision most plausibly sits — Freight/Trucking & Intermodal for trailers — with the overlap flagged rather than the asset being counted twice into Warehousing/Logistics as well.

**Double-counting risks identified and how they were handled:**
- **Rental-owned vs. contractor-owned equipment** (§2, visible double-count note): a rented excavator or MEWP appears in the Rental/Leasing table as part of the rental company's fleet, and operationally on a construction owner-operator's job site, but is not double-counted as an *owned* asset in both places — the two tables are additive only for genuinely distinct populations, and I did not attempt to sum them into one grand total.
- **Trailers vs. chassis vs. containers**: chassis (700,000+, IANA) are a distinct asset from the marine containers they carry; no reliable US container count was found, so the Freight/Intermodal totals in §3–§4 explicitly exclude an unconfirmed container count (a labeled gap, not a silent omission).
- **Forklifts in manufacturing vs. warehousing/3PL**: grouped as one vertical because the underlying asset (a Class 1–5 lift truck) and the buying pattern are functionally identical whether the site is a distribution center or a factory floor — a manufacturing-only vs. logistics-only split was not available and is flagged as a conflation rather than an invented split.
- **Waste hauler containers**: flagged in §4 as a hybrid — the hauler is conventionally an owner-operator of its collection business, but the containers themselves behave economically like a rental fleet; a KPI researcher should study both utilization metrics (rental-style) and route/service-cost metrics (owner-operator-style) for this vertical.

3. **No current ITA-published forklift/lift-truck installed-base figure**, and **no rented-vs-owned split for forklifts** (i.e., how big the short-term forklift-rental sub-market is relative to owned fleets). My 2.5–3.5M estimate is a shipment-run-rate extrapolation, not a primary count.

5. **No unit count for roll-off/dumpster containers, temporary fencing panels, or scaffolding components** anywhere in the US — only revenue figures for these rental categories were found.
6. **No unit count for utility/telecom field assets** as a discrete "software-managed asset" category — this vertical may be better modeled as adjacent to Enterprise Asset Management (EAM) systems than to fleet/asset-tracking SaaS, but this framing is inference, not sourced.

8. **True per-asset telematics/tracking penetration rates are largely unavailable** for every vertical except cargo-carrying units (Berg Insight's ~20% figure is the one hard number in this report). All other "penetration" figures are either market-value shares (not unit-count shares) or Low-confidence survey stats.
9. **Gas cylinder and keg population in the US** — no unit count found at all.
10. **No quantified rental-vs-owned split for any vertical studied** — every note in §2–§4 about "the majority is owned" or "a smaller rental sub-segment exists" is a directional, unsourced judgment call, not a measured percentage. This is the most consequential gap for the buyer-type framework specifically, since it determines the relative sizing of the two tables' overlap.

## 12. Summary of confidence

The most defensible, citable numbers in this entire report are: (a) Berg Insight's ~20% tracking penetration and 11.1M→27M installed-base trajectory for cargo-carrying units; (b) IPAF's 857,000-unit US MEWP rental fleet; (c) IANA's 700,000+ chassis pool; (d) AHA's 913,136 staffed hospital beds; (e) USDA's 1.9 million US farms; and (f) NER/NICB's $300M–$1B annual equipment-theft-loss range. Every other headline count in this report (total trailers, total forklifts, total construction equipment, total roll-off containers, total pallets, and every rented-vs-owned split) is either a dated historical anchor, a reasoned extrapolation labeled as such, or a vendor market-research figure that should be independently re-verified before being used in an external-facing sizing document.
