# Clear writing rules

## Structure

**1. One thought per piece.** Decide the single takeaway and make everything serve it. If you cannot name the takeaway, stop writing and go think.

**2. Main point above, context below.** The point and the ask come first. Everything else sits underneath. The reader can stop after the first block and still act.

**3. You do not have to answer every question in the main body.** Follow-ups and appendices exist. The main points being unmistakably clear beats being complete.

**4. Number your points when you are stuck.** One idea per line, numbered. Numbering forces you to decide the order of importance, and it lets a reader disagree with item three instead of the whole thing.

## Word choice

**5. Use the plain, short, everyday word.** Of two words that mean the same thing, use the shorter. "Help", not "assistance". No jargon, scientific term or foreign phrase where everyday English exists. A difficult topic does not license difficult vocabulary.

Avoid unless the reader used them first: circularity, coherence, causal chain, denominator, numerator, cohort, survivorship bias, attribution, instrument as a verb, normalise, confounded, inherent, structural, orthogonal, delta. If one is genuinely needed, use it once and say what it means in the same breath.

**6. Active voice.** Name who did what.

**7. Never use a metaphor or figure of speech you are used to seeing in print.** If you recalled the phrase rather than chose it, cut it.

**8. Fancy writing is inconsiderate.** Writing to impress makes the reader do extra work so you can look clever.

**9. Complexity on purpose, never by accident.** There are moments for a long sentence or an unusual word. Choose them, and know why.

## Cutting

**10. Every word must earn its place.** If two words do the same job, one goes. If a word can be cut, cut it. Includes qualifiers and redundant pairs: very, quite, sort of, generally, each and every.

**11. Cut past the point of comfort, then check what broke.** Delete a phrase. If the piece still works without it, it was never needed. Pretend there is a hard character limit.

**12. Fast first draft, then a long edit.** Most of the edit is cutting. Never present the first draft.

## Honesty

**13. Drop "I think".** "I think Y is not going well" becomes "Y is not going well". What still carries a hedge should be genuinely up for debate.

**14. Plain writing keeps you honest.** Complicated prose hides missing substance. Say nothing simply and everyone can see it, including you.

## Clarity checks

**15. If words can be read more than one way, they will be read the worst way.** Run one pass hunting for sentences readable two ways.

**16. If you have to repeat yourself, you were not clear the first time.** The exception is a genuinely new idea, which may need repeating for years.

## Overrides

**Sound like a person.** Keep the warmth. It costs few words.

**Break any rule sooner than write something clumsy.**
